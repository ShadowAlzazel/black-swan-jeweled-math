Conformal jewels

Tile the input plane z = x + iy, then carry every tile through a complex function w = f(z) = u + iv. The graph has four real coordinates (x, y, u, v); the two linked planes let you follow the same tile through that space. Start with Rectangles → Exponential → golden ratio, then move the input shear.

Rings, sectors, spirals

ex+iy = exeiy: a vertical line becomes a circle of radius ex and a horizontal line becomes a ray at angle y. A rectangular grid therefore becomes rings and sectors. Shear the input so its horizontal lines tilt, y = s x + b, and the rays become logarithmic spirals r = e(θ − b)/s.

Euler's identity turns into construction rules: ez+iπ = −ez (half-turn), ez+2πi = ez (one complete wrap), and ez+log c = c ez (scaling by c). The step buttons apply them to the selected tile and report f(z + Δ)/f(z) exactly.

Feigenbaum scale choices

δ ≈ 4.669 measures shrinking parameter intervals; α ≈ −2.503 rescales space near the critical orbit. Here both are only geometric ring ratios. Because α is negative, α = |α|eiπ and log α = log|α| + iπ: one ring outward plus Euler's half-turn. The α jewel has that twist built in, so it is invariant under w ↦ αw. Press Zoom × c with α selected: the view grows by |α| and turns by π, and the picture repeats. Two levels make one full wrap: α² = |α|²e2πi.

The dynamical roles of δ and α are computed separately in experiments/04_complex_spectre/feigenbaum_renormalization.py.

Folding and coverings

A nonlinear image need not remain a non-overlapping tiling. On one strip of height 2π, eᶻ keeps the tiles separate (multiplicity 1). Squaring a window symmetric about 0 folds z and −z together (multiplicity 2). Click a point of the output plane to count its preimages. Hat and pinwheel patches are aperiodic, so their eᶻ jewels show a seam along the negative real axis, where the top and bottom of the strip meet.

The disk map tanh(z/2) sends the strip |Im z| < π/2 onto the unit disk. Columns become hyperbolic geodesics, rows become curves ending at ±1, and a step of log c is a hyperbolic translation of length log c.

Elliptic lattice jewels

The Weierstrass ℘-function is built from a lattice and is doubly periodic, so the lattice is part of the function. It is even, so one period cell covers its image twice. Here ℘ sums the lattice series near the reduced point and adds the Eisenstein tail 3G4z² + 5G6z⁴; it satisfies ℘′² = 4℘³ − g2℘ − g3 to about 10−6.

Each tile edge is sampled before mapping (at least the chosen number of points, more on long edges), so curved image boundaries stay curved.

Hat patches come from the hat authors' BSD-3-Clause substitution source (Smith, Myers, Kaplan, Goodman-Strauss); see third-party notices. Pinwheel patches come from this repository's Conway–Radin substitution. Background: NIST DLMF chapter 4 (elementary functions) and chapter 23 (Weierstrass elliptic functions).

Everything runs locally. These are geometric pictures, not claims about satisfiability or complexity.
