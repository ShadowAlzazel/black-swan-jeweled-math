Energy jewels

Give every clause the weight z. A formula becomes one polynomial, Z(z) = Σx zE(x) = Σk Nk zk, where Nk counts the assignments that violate exactly k clauses. Z(0) is the number of solutions. Unlike the field of Experiment 04, Z is a genuine polynomial, so Cauchy's formula and the argument principle hold, and its zeros belong to the formula.

Count instead of search

Z(0) = N0 is the number of solutions and Z(1) = 2n counts every assignment. Every coefficient is an exact count, computed here by brute force over all 2n assignments and cross-checked against tensor-network contraction in experiments/05_counting_jewels/counting.py.

Cauchy's formula gives N0 as the average of Z over any circle around 0. Move r: the average stays N0 exactly.

The cost is conserved

Nothing here is a shortcut. Every value Z(z) is itself a sum over all 2n assignments, so a contour integral that counts solutions costs as much as counting them. Contraction makes that sum cheaper only when the formula's graph is narrow, as with geometric formulas.

A legitimate winding number

Experiment 04's field FΦ was not holomorphic, so its winding numbers counted vortices minus antivortices, and its zeros were not solutions. Z is a polynomial, so the winding number of Z(reiθ) around 0 equals the number of zeros inside |z| < r. The compressed radius keeps every winding, because it only rescales distance from 0.

Reading the zeros

Near the origin, Z ≈ N0 + N1z, so the nearest zero sits close to −N0/N1: few solutions per near-solution means a zero close to 0. An unsatisfiable formula has N0 = 0, so z = 0 itself is a zero.
